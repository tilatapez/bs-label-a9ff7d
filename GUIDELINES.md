# Beyond Sarcasm: Annotation Guidelines

**Task owner:** Aseel Salameh (ARI 510, Fall 2026). **Contact:** aseels@umich.edu, or message me on the course Discord.
**Time needed:** about 20 seconds per clip. There are 99 clips, about 30–40 minutes in total.


## 1. Your job in one paragraph

You will listen to short audio clips (about 10 seconds each) of people talking in **English** or **Arabic**. For each clip, choose **one** label that describes the speaker's stance: are they being sarcastic, and if so, what kind of sarcasm? Then say how sure you are. There are no trick questions and no "correct" answer key. I want your honest impression of how the speaker comes across.

## 2. The four labels

| # | Label | Pick it when the speaker… | Typical voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Non-Sarcastic** | means what they say. No clear sarcasm. Includes sincere jokes, excitement, complaints and anger that are said straight. | Matches the words |
| 2 | **Affiliative sarcasm** | says the opposite of what they mean (or exaggerates) **to connect**: playful teasing, joking with a friend or family member, affectionate mockery, flirting. | Smiling voice, laughter, warm, light |
| 3 | **Aggressive sarcasm** | says the opposite of what they mean **to attack or criticize**: mocking, irritated, contemptuous, insulting. | Sharp, cutting, stressed words, sneering |
| 4 | **Distancing sarcasm** | says the opposite of what they mean **to pull away**: dismissive, resigned, "whatever", "sure, great", bored, checked out. | Flat, tired, drawn out, low energy |

**Why these labels:** sarcasm is not one thing. The same words ("oh, great job") can tease a friend, attack someone, or shrug something off. I am testing whether the voice carries that difference.

## 3. How to decide (step by step)

1. **Play the full clip.** Replay it if needed.
2. **Is there sarcasm?** Ask: does the speaker mean something different from (often the opposite of) what the words or tone literally say? Signs: exaggerated praise for something bad, a mismatch between happy words and an annoyed voice, mock enthusiasm, an "oh *sure*" tone.
   - No, or you can't hear any → **1 Non-Sarcastic**.
   - Plain anger, plain complaining, or a plain joke is **not** sarcasm on its own.
3. **If yes, who is it aimed at, and how does it feel?**
   - Warm, playful, the relationship seems fine → **2 Affiliative**.
   - Meant to hurt, criticize or put someone down → **3 Aggressive**.
   - Giving up, brushing it off, not wanting to engage → **4 Distancing**.
4. **Two sarcasm types seem to fit?** Pick the one that is stronger in the **voice**, and set confidence to 1 or 2.
5. **Rate your confidence:** 1 = guessing, 2 = fairly sure, 3 = very sure.
6. **Can't judge?** Tick "I can't judge this clip" only if there is no usable speech (music only, noise, crosstalk). Don't tick it just because the clip is hard; guess and set confidence to 1.

**Clips are short and have no context.** That is part of the task. Judge only what you hear in these 10 seconds.

## 4. Arabic clips if you don't speak Arabic

About half the clips are in Arabic (Levantine or Egyptian dialect). **Please label them anyway.** The tool asks whether you understand Arabic and saves that with your labels, so I can analyze your answers separately. Your labels are useful: one of my research questions is whether sarcasm can be heard from the voice alone.

Listen for the **delivery**, not the words:
- **Pitch:** big swoops or a sing-song, exaggerated rise or fall often mark sarcasm. A very flat, monotone line on emotional words often marks distancing.
- **Speed and stretching:** words stretched out ("maaaashi") or said very slowly can signal mock agreement.
- **Laughter or a smile in the voice:** usually points to affiliative.
- **Sharp, clipped, loud stress:** usually points to aggressive.
- **Sighs, trailing off, low energy:** usually points to distancing.
- **Repeated words** in a flat or mocking voice (e.g. "ماشي ماشي ماشي", "okay okay okay") are often sarcastic.

If you honestly hear nothing marked in the voice, choose **Non-Sarcastic** with confidence 1.

## 6. Tricky cases

| Case | What to do |
|---|---|
| Angry but sincere ("this is so frustrating") | Non-Sarcastic. Anger alone isn't sarcasm. |
| A joke or comedy sketch with no sarcasm in it | Non-Sarcastic. |
| Teasing that also has a bite | Pick the stronger feel. Mostly playful → Affiliative, mostly cutting → Aggressive. Lower your confidence. |
| Sarcasm about a situation, not a person ("oh, *perfect*, it's raining") | Usually Distancing (resigned) or Aggressive (irritated). Go by the voice. |
| Several people talking | Label the most prominent speaker. |
| Background music or laughter | It was mostly filtered out. Ignore whatever remains and judge the voice. |
